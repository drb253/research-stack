# Registers (target tone)

Use these when the user asks for a specific tone (`--tone <name>`), or when no writing sample is given
and the register is obvious from context. A register sets **surface choices only** - sentence length,
person, contractions, jargon, warmth, structure. It never licenses inventing facts, and a writing sample
always outranks a register. If the user gives both a sample and a tone, follow the sample and say so.

---

## academic — journal articles, theses, lab reports, literature reviews
- Neutral and precise; hedge only where the evidence is uncertain. No contractions. Third person;
  "we" is fine for a team. Define a term on first use.
- No rhetorical questions, no exclamations, no second person. Longer sentences are allowed if a reader
  can hold the clause.
- Keep every citation and hedge untouched. Cut importance puffery (§10) and vague sourcing (§23) hard.

## technical — docs, READMEs, API/CLI guides, runbooks
- Imperative for steps ("Run the build"). Second person ("you"). Short sentences, concrete nouns,
  exact command and path names.
- One idea per line. No marketing adjectives (§14). Never invent a flag, endpoint, or version.

## professional — business email, memos, reports, proposals
- Plain and direct. Contractions fine. Lead with the ask or the decision, then the support.
- Short paragraphs, warm but not chatty. No "thrilled to announce," no exclamation stacks.

## casual — blog posts, personal essays, newsletters, internal chat
- Varied sentence length; fragments and asides allowed. Contractions, first person, humor, digressions.
- Keep the writer's slang and odd details. Do not sand off personality for tidiness.

## social — X/Twitter, LinkedIn, short product posts
- One clear idea. Punchy, but not the LinkedIn tics: no broetry, no "Here's what I learned 🧵", no
  one-line-per-paragraph padding, no engagement-bait closer.
- Emoji only if the writer's own voice uses them.

## journalistic — news and features
- Inverted pyramid: most important first. Active voice. Attribution named, never "experts say" (§12).
- No first person unless it is a column. Concrete detail over abstraction.

## reply — thread replies, email replies, comments
- Decide first: lead with the decision or answer. Drop background the reader already has (§25).
- Keep only the reasoning that would change whether the reader agrees, plus any link they need to act.
- The diagnosis and the proof belong in the document that follows, not in the reply.

---

## How to apply a register

1. If the user names a register, adopt it. If they paste a writing sample, the sample wins over any
   register - match it instead.
2. Apply the register to surface choices only (length, person, contractions, jargon, warmth). It never
   touches facts, numbers, names, or citations.
3. State the register you used at the top of the **What changed** list, e.g.
   "Register: professional (no sample given)."
4. A register is a starting default, not a cage. If the writer's subject clearly needs another
   register (a joke in a memo that must stay), keep it and note the exception.
